Mock 1 — System Design60 min

A new case, different from Day 10's — to force you to apply the framework from memory, not repeat an already-prepared answer.

"Design an agent that helps a bank's customers resolve questions about their financial products and initiate simple requests (e.g., blocking a card), escalating any irreversible operation to a human."

Walk through Day 06's 16 steps out loud, with a timer. When you finish, self-assess with the same Day 10 rubric.

Mock 2 — Composition Case45 min

An ambiguous problem, no technical hints. Apply Day 09's 9-step framework.

"A mid-sized company's HR team gets hundreds of repetitive employee questions about internal policies every week, via email and Slack."

Pay special attention to not jumping to "Architecture" before step 7 — that's the mistake this mock is designed to catch.

Mock 3 — General Manager30 min

Answer each question out loud, timed, in under 2 minutes. Use the stories from your Day 13 bank.

  • Why FDE?
  • Why AI?
  • Why now?
  • How do you work with customers?
  • Describe a difficult architecture decision.
  • Describe a production incident.
  • Tell me about something you had to learn quickly.
Mock 4 — Technical Deep Dive30 min

Answer each question out loud before opening the suggested angle — these are direct questions from across the whole bootcamp, rapid-fire format.

Why RAG vs fine-tuning?
See suggested angle
Day 01 RAG queries at runtime (easy to keep current); fine-tuning bakes knowledge into the weights (expensive, hard to update).
How do you avoid prompt injection?
See suggested angle
Day 04 Treat all external content as data, never instruction; minimum-scope tools so that even if the injection "works," the damage is bounded.
How do you limit an agent's actions?
See suggested angle
Days 02 and 04 Minimum-scope tools by design + human approval for high-impact actions — architecture, not prompting.
What happens if the LLM fails?
See suggested angle
Day 05 Model fallback to a secondary provider/model, circuit breaker, and an honest degraded response instead of hanging.
How do you evaluate an agent's quality?
See suggested angle
Day 05 Offline golden set before deploy + online sampling of real conversations (human review or LLM-as-judge).
When do you use multi-agent?
See suggested angle
Day 02 When there are heterogeneous knowledge domains with different security scopes — not as a default because of perceived complexity.
Where do you store the state?
See suggested angle
Days 02 and 08 DynamoDB (or similar) for keyed conversation state; RDS for relational business data — never mix the two.
How do you design observability?
See suggested angle
Day 05 A distributed trace spanning LLM decisions + tool calls + orchestration, plus AI-specific metrics (tokens, latency, hallucination rate).
You're ready if...

You can solve, unaided, a problem like:

"Design an enterprise AI agent capable of interacting with customers, querying corporate information, executing actions on internal systems, and escalating to humans, meeting security, compliance, availability, and observability requirements."

And your answer walks through this full chain, without skipping a link:

Business Problem → Requirements → Architecture → Agent → Tools → Data/RAG → Security → Integrations → Reliability → Observability → Scalability → Cost → Trade-offs → Rollout

"Don't prepare to answer AI questions. Prepare to design and build enterprise solutions with AI."
