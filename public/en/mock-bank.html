Mock 1

System Design

60 minutes · interviewer → candidate · technical and communication feedback
Clarify→ Requirements→ Architecture→ Security→ Resilience→ Rollout

Full 16-step framework in Day 06. Already-solved cases: enterprise voice agent (Day 10), banking agent (Day 14).

TipThe interviewer evaluates the process as much as the result — spending 3-5 min clarifying before drawing a box beats quickly designing the wrong thing.

Additional cases to keep practicing:

  • "Design an agent that helps doctors summarize clinical histories and suggest billing codes, without making autonomous clinical decisions."
  • "Design an agent that manages hotel reservations — inquiries, changes, and cancellations — integrated with the existing PMS."
  • "Design an internal agent that resolves first-level technical support tickets for employees at a 5,000-person company."
Mock 2

Composition Case

45 minutes · ambiguous problem · structuring · prioritization · solution
Problem→ Users→ Pain Points→ Business Value→ Processes→ ...→ Architecture→ Implementation

Full 9-step framework in Day 09. Already-solved cases: logistics reconciliation (Day 09), HR FAQ (Day 14).

TipArchitecture appears at step 8 of 9 — if you get there before minute 20, you probably skipped something.

Additional cases to keep practicing:

  • "A SaaS company's sales team takes 3 days on average to respond to qualified inbound leads."
  • "A fintech's customers abandon the onboarding process 40% of the time before completing identity verification."
  • "A manufacturing company's finance team spends 15 hours a week reconciling vendor invoices against purchase orders."
Mock 3

General Manager

30 minutes · behavioral · <2 min per answer

Answer using the STAR + Architecture Context format (Day 13) — Situation, Task, Action, Result, plus the specific technical detail. Your Day 13 bank of 8 stories should cover all of these.

  • Why this company?
  • Why FDE?
  • Why AI?
  • Why now?
  • How do you work with customers?
  • Describe a difficult architecture decision.
  • Describe a production incident.
  • Tell me about something you had to learn quickly.
TipDon't look for the "perfect" story — look for the most specific one. A concrete technical detail beats an impressive achievement told generically.
Mock 4

Technical Deep Dive

30 minutes · direct questions · rapid-fire format

Answer out loud before opening each suggested angle.

Why RAG vs fine-tuning?
See suggested angle
Day 01 RAG queries at runtime (easy to keep current); fine-tuning bakes knowledge into the weights (expensive, hard to update).
How do you avoid prompt injection?
See suggested angle
Day 04 Treat all external content as data, never instruction; minimum-scope tools to bound the damage even if the injection "works."
How do you limit an agent's actions?
See suggested angle
Days 02 and 04 Minimum-scope tools + human approval for high-impact actions — architecture, not prompting.
What happens if the LLM fails?
See suggested angle
Day 05 Model fallback to a secondary provider/model, circuit breaker, and an honest degraded response.
How do you evaluate an agent's quality?
See suggested angle
Day 05 Offline golden set before deploy + online sampling of real conversations (human or LLM-as-judge).
When do you use multi-agent?
See suggested angle
Day 02 Heterogeneous knowledge domains with different security scopes — not by default.
Where do you store the state?
See suggested angle
Days 02 and 08 DynamoDB for conversation state; RDS for relational business data.
How do you design observability?
See suggested angle
Day 05 A distributed trace spanning LLM + tool calls + orchestration, plus AI-specific metrics.
