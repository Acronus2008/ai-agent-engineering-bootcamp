Communication & Executive Presence

A framework for structuring any technical answer, and the exact phrasing to carry it through an architecture interview.

~90 min Prerequisite: Day 11 Connects to: Day 13 — Behavioral
Today's objective

Have a reusable mold for any technical answer — so you don't have to improvise the structure every time, only the content.

The framework: Context → Decision → Reason → Trade-off → Result
Context→ Decision→ Reason→ Trade-off→ Result

Use it to explain any technical decision in 60-90 seconds: set the context, say what you decided, why, what you sacrificed by choosing it, and what happened.

Applied example (Day 08)Context: "We needed to orchestrate a 4-step refund flow with compensations." Decision: "I chose Step Functions instead of coordinating the Saga manually with Kafka." Reason: "Because we needed per-step retry and error handling, with clear state visibility." Trade-off: "We gave up some flexibility compared to a pure event-based approach, in exchange for less custom orchestration code." Result: "The flow ended up auditable step by step, and implementation time went down."
The other communication pieces
Elevator Pitch

30-60 seconds, no filler

Your answer to "why this role, why this company, why now" should fit in under a minute, with a structure: who you are technically, what connects you to the role, and what you specifically bring that isn't generic.

Explaining a diagram

From the outside in, naming the failure you anticipated

Start with the user, follow the flow in the order it happens, and at each box briefly mention what happens if that piece fails — it shows you designed with failure in mind, not just the happy path.

Executive presentations

The conclusion first, the detail after

An executive isn't interested in how you got to the conclusion before knowing what it is. Start with the result/recommendation, and have the technical detail ready if they ask "why?".

Key phrases for architecture interviews
  • "Let me clarify the requirements first."
  • "There are two main trade-offs here."
  • "I would start with a synchronous approach, but move to async if X."
  • "The bottleneck is likely to be..."
  • "I would introduce asynchronous processing if the volume grows beyond..."
  • "The main risk with this approach is..."
  • "To keep this idempotent, I'd use a key based on..."
  • "Let's walk through what happens on the failure path."
  • "I'll assume X for now — let me know if that's not the case."
  • "The reason I'm choosing this over Y is..."
Practical exercise

Explain the Day 10 diagram out loud

Take the voice agent diagram (Day 10) and explain it out loud, using the Context→Decision→Reason→Trade-off→Result framework for at least two of the decisions you made (e.g., choosing latency over model "intelligence").

See an example opening

"Let me walk you through the architecture. Context: the company needed a voice agent handling both inquiries and actions, with human handoff for high-risk cases. Decision: I chose a faster, smaller model with tightly-scoped RAG over a larger, more capable one. Reason: in voice, perceived latency matters more than in chat — a two-second silence feels like a failure. Trade-off: we accept slightly less nuanced answers in exchange for responsiveness. Result: average response time stayed under 1.5 seconds, which was the hard requirement."

Related interview questions
  • Walk me through a technical decision you made recently, and why.
  • How do you adjust your communication style between a developer and an executive audience?
  • Give me a 60-second pitch on why you want this role.
  • Explain this architecture to me as if I were a non-technical stakeholder.
  • What's the biggest risk in the design you just proposed?
