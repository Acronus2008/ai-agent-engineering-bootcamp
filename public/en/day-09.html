Composition Case

The other half of the technical interview: you're not given a technical problem, you're given an ambiguous business problem. The skill being evaluated is problem decomposition.

~90 min Prerequisite: Day 06 Connects to: Day 10 — Mock System Design
Today's objective

Distinguish a Composition Case from a System Design case: here the problem doesn't come predefined, it comes as a complaint or a business observation. Your job is to turn that into something actionable before thinking about architecture.

The framework
  1. Problem What you're given is almost always a symptom, not the real problem. Rephrase it in your own words before moving on.
  2. Users Who lives with the problem — there's almost always more than one group, with interests that don't always align.
  3. Pain Points Concrete friction for each user group — not "it's slow," but "user X loses Y minutes doing Z manually."
  4. Business Value Connect the pain point to a metric the business cares about: time, cost, retention, risk.
  5. Processes Map the current (as-is) process before proposing anything — without this, any solution is a guess.
  6. Automation Opportunities Within the mapped process, where does automation/AI actually help — and where shouldn't it be touched?
  7. Risks What can go wrong with the proposed automation: technical, business, compliance.
  8. Architecture Only here — the technical sketch of the solution (this is where you connect to Day 06).
  9. Implementation A phased plan: what ships first (MVP), what comes next, how success is measured.
Composition Case vs System Design
The key differenceSystem Design assumes the problem is already defined and evaluates technical depth. Composition Case evaluates whether you can turn business ambiguity into a well-defined problem — the architecture is just the final output, not the focus of the evaluation.
Practice case

Prompt: "A logistics company's warehouse staff lose 2 hours a day reconciling discrepancies between the WMS and the carriers' systems."

Walk through the full framework out loud before looking at the suggested approach.

See suggested approach

Problem

The prompt gives a symptom (2 hours lost). The real problem could be: lack of real-time visibility into discrepancies, or an inherently manual reconciliation process with no single source of truth.

Users

Warehouse staff (run the reconciliation), logistics supervisors (need accurate reports), end customers (indirectly affected by delays not caught in time).

Pain Points

Warehouse: manually cross-checking two systems that don't sync. Supervisors: reports with stale data. Customers: shipments with incorrect status.

Business Value

2h/day × warehouse staff = quantifiable direct labor cost; plus, discrepancies not caught in time generate customer complaints and carrier penalties.

Processes

As-is: export data from both systems, compare manually in a spreadsheet, flag discrepancies, fix them by hand in the WMS.

Automation Opportunities

An agent can automate the comparison and flagging of discrepancies — but the decision of how to resolve each discrepancy (is it a data-entry error, a lost shipment, a carrier issue?) probably needs human judgment at first, with the agent learning patterns over time.

Risks

False positives/negatives in detection erode the team's trust. Auto-correcting without supervision could hide real systemic errors instead of exposing them.

Architecture (brief)

An agent with two read-only tools (WMS API, carrier API) that compares and generates a prioritized discrepancy report — no automatic writes at first.

Implementation

Phase 1 (MVP): automatic detection and reporting, no auto-correction — cuts the time to "find" the problem. Phase 2: resolution suggestions based on historical patterns, with human approval. Phase 3: auto-resolution for the highest-confidence cases.

Related interview questions
  • How do you avoid jumping straight to "let's build an agent" before understanding the problem?
  • What do you do when the "problem" you're given is actually a symptom of something deeper?
  • How do you prioritize between several possible automation opportunities?
  • How do you communicate a risk without sounding like you're saying no to the idea?
  • What's the central difference between a Composition Case and a System Design case?
